# Asking

Read at the start of stage 1, with `references/frame.md`. It holds for every stage.

## What to ask

Ask only what changes the output, and only while the answer can still change something: a question made moot by an earlier decision is not asked. Two kinds:

- A fact only the person has (their numbers, their users, their systems, what happens today), when `brief.md` does not already hold it. A fact is what is true today. Up to three in one turn, numbered, with no recommended answer, since a fact cannot be agreed to. The stage 1 question on what the subject counts on, when asked, always goes alone.
- A choice: anything about what should be built or done (a product, a system, a process), with a real trade-off (two defensible answers with different costs), or any value on a risky path (money, health data, personal data, regulation or a change people cannot undo). One per turn, with a recommended answer and one line on why, because several choices at once get answered by agreeing to all of them.

A turn never mixes facts and a choice. Never ask the person to confirm a summary; the only confirmation is the verdict.

## What not to ask

An item with one obvious default, an edge case the person is unlikely to know (except on a risky path, where the behaviour is asked), wording, or a screen detail becomes `A-NN` in `brief.md` with the proposed default, the reason and `Confirmed? n`. Stage 4 shows every one with the verdict, and the person corrects any of them in the same answer; a correction becomes `D-NN`.

- "I don't know": record an assumption (the recommendation for a choice, a proposed default for a fact) and move on, with no follow-up.
- A fact someone else holds ("I'd have to ask Ines"): record it in `brief.md` under Open questions with `Held by: <who>`, tagged `open`, and carry on to the verdict. Never ask who knows, and never pause to wait for it; the person can say "resume" later with the answer. Stage 4 lists it under Evidence to collect.
- A rule the person states is a decision: record it as `D-NN` the moment they say it, so the handoff cannot drop it. Never present a proposed default as a fact about their business.

## Turn format

```
Stage 3 of 5 · Grill · 6 of ~12 decisions made

<one choice, or up to three numbered facts>

Recommended: <answer, for a choice only>
Checked: <finding IDs and date, or "nothing time-sensitive">
```

`~12` is your running estimate for the stage. For a choice, offer the recommendation and up to two alternatives. After each answer, one line on what changed, then the next turn.

Example. The person says: "An app for small bakeries in Brazil to take pre-orders by WhatsApp and charge a deposit by Pix." `Stage 1 of 5 · Frame · 0 of ~4 decisions made`, then two numbered facts: is anyone ordering this way today, and what a no-show costs. The next turn asks, on its own, which prices, fees, limits or rules the idea counts on. `Checked: nothing time-sensitive`.
